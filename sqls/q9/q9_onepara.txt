Q9. THE BUDGET
===============
Finance can release BDT 70,000 for next month. It must cover all one-time
costs and the salary of any new agents for that month. How would you use
it, and what do you expect it to return?

Assumptions: Fixed agent = BDT 650/day (16,900 ÷ 26), i.e. BDT 19,500/month; Variable breakeven is 25 deliveries/day (650 = 300 + 14d), and every understaffed hub operates above that, so Fixed is cheaper; avg delivery charge = BDT 65 (orders table); one-time hiring costs are from the missing "costs" tab — three scenarios (BDT 5k / 10k / 15.5k per agent) are used instead of an invented figure; a 50% Late Dispatch reduction at Riverside is a target, not a guarantee.

Answer:

The BDT 70,000 budget cannot fund the 8-agent request — salary alone would be BDT 156,000 — and at any realistic one-time cost it supports at most 2 Fixed hires (2 × 19,500 salary = BDT 39,000, leaving BDT 31,000 for one-time costs and operations), but it does not need to fund more, because the company's real problem is distribution, not headcount: Lakeview sits at 56.2% utilisation with 3 surplus agents (Q2/Q7) while Hillcrest runs at 95.9% and Greenfield at 88.6%, both above the 85% target, so the plan's first move is to transfer 3 Fixed agents from Lakeview to Greenfield (2) and Hillcrest (1) at zero incremental cost — these agents go from delivering 20.4 orders/day each at Lakeview to 32–35/day at the busier hubs (Q1 W2 averages), a net gain of 37.4 deliveries/day or 1,122/month worth BDT 72,930 in revenue, while Lakeview's cost per delivery drops from BDT 31.06 to ~BDT 22.15 (−29%) simply by removing idle overhead (Q6); the second move hires 2 new Fixed agents — one for Hillcrest (still short by 1 after the transfer, the most urgent gap in the network) and one as a buffer at Riverside, whose +1 staffing gap is entirely caused by 312 Late Dispatch holds per week inflating its attempts-per-order to 1.351 vs the ~1.075 company average (Q3/Q7), and would flip to a surplus of −1 if dispatch were fixed (2,245 orders × 1.075 = 2,413 attempts → 11 agents needed vs 12 active) — these 2 hires add 1,701 deliveries/month (Hillcrest: 34 attempts ÷ 1.078 = 31.5 del/day × 30 = 945; Riverside: 34 ÷ 1.351 = 25.2 × 30 = 756), generating BDT 110,565 in revenue against BDT 39,000 salary, a net of BDT 71,565 with a payback period of 10.6 days; the third move invests whatever budget remains after one-time costs (BDT 0–21,000 depending on the per-agent one-time figure) in a Riverside dispatch improvement programme targeting the 312 Late Dispatch holds, which currently waste BDT 25,650/month in avoidable re-attempts (312 × BDT 19.12 per attempt × 4.3 weeks) — halving them frees 671 attempts/month, producing 624 additional deliveries (671 ÷ 1.075) worth BDT 40,560 plus BDT 12,830 in recovered agent time, a combined BDT 53,390/month benefit against a one-time spend of at most BDT 21,000. Altogether, the three moves yield 3,447 additional deliveries and BDT 236,885 in monthly revenue at a cost of no more than BDT 60,000 (salary + dispatch programme), for a first-month net return of at least BDT 176,885 — a 253% ROI on the BDT 70,000 budget — and from month 2 onward the recurring cost is just BDT 39,000 salary against returns exceeding BDT 200,000; every hub moves into the 72–84% healthy utilisation band, capacity headroom doubles from 7.3% to 15.6%, and the single largest source of operational waste in the network is addressed at its root rather than papered over with headcount.
