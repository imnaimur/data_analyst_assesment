Q9. THE BUDGET — STRATEGIC ASSESSMENT
=======================================

Question
--------
Finance can release BDT 70,000 for next month. It must cover all one-
time costs and the salary of any new agents for that month. How would
you use it, and what do you expect it to return?


===============================================================================
1. THE KEY OPERATIONAL PROBLEM
===============================================================================

The company does not have one problem — it has two, and they require
different treatments:

PROBLEM A — CAPACITY MISMATCH (between hubs, not company-wide)
  Three hubs (Greenfield, Hillcrest, Riverside) are short by a combined
  5 agents at the planning target. Two hubs (Lakeview, Brookside) have
  a combined 4 surplus agents. Company-wide, the net gap is just +1
  (Q7/Q8). The capacity problem is a distribution problem, not an
  absolute shortage.

  Evidence:
  - Greenfield: 88.6% utilisation in W2 with 10 agents; needs 12 (Q2/Q7)
  - Hillcrest: 95.9% utilisation in W2 with 9 agents; needs 11 (Q2/Q7)
  - Lakeview: 56.2% utilisation with 11 agents; needs only 8 (Q2/Q7)
  - Brookside: 71.5% utilisation with 11 agents; needs only 10 (Q2/Q7)

PROBLEM B — OPERATIONAL WASTE AT RIVERSIDE
  Riverside's 874 holds in Week 2 — 563 of them agent-side (64.4%) —
  consume 18.4% of the hub's total attempts on avoidable re-delivery
  cycles (Q3). This inflates Riverside's attempts-per-order to 1.351
  versus the company average of 1.075, creating a phantom staffing gap
  of +1 that would become a surplus of -1 if dispatch were fixed (Q8).
  The wasted agent time costs an estimated BDT 10,753 per week (see
  Section 4).

WHY BOTH MATTER FOR THE BUDGET
  If the BDT 70,000 goes entirely to hiring, it addresses Problem A
  but leaves Problem B untouched. Riverside continues to burn agent
  time on avoidable re-attempts, and within a quarter the hub will
  request yet another agent. The budget should address both — staffing
  the genuine gaps AND reducing the operational waste.


===============================================================================
2. POSSIBLE INTERVENTIONS — COMPARED
===============================================================================

OPTION 1: HIRE NEW AGENTS ONLY
  What it does: adds headcount at understaffed hubs.
  Cost: BDT 19,500/month per Fixed agent (650/day × 30 days).
         BDT 22,440/month per Variable agent at 32 deliveries/day
         (300 + 14 × 32 = 748/day × 30 days).
  Budget constraint:

  One-time cost/agent | Max Fixed hires | Remaining budget
  --------------------+-----------------+-----------------
  BDT 0               |               3 |        BDT 11,500
  BDT 5,000           |               2 |        BDT 21,000
  BDT 10,000          |               2 |        BDT 11,000
  BDT 15,000          |               2 |         BDT 1,000

  The budget CANNOT fund more than 3 Fixed hires even at zero one-time
  cost. The 8-agent request (8 × 19,500 = BDT 156,000 salary alone)
  exceeds the budget by BDT 86,000 — before any one-time costs. Even
  the Q7 gap of 5 (5 × 19,500 = BDT 97,500) is unaffordable.

  Advantage: directly adds delivery capacity where needed.
  Limitation: cannot fill more than 2–3 positions; does nothing for
    Riverside's dispatch problem; surplus at Lakeview remains idle.

OPTION 2: REALLOCATE EXISTING AGENTS FROM SURPLUS HUBS
  What it does: moves 3 agents from Lakeview (which has 3 surplus) to
    Greenfield (2) and Hillcrest (1), filling 3 of the 5-position gap.
  Cost: BDT 0 in additional salary — these agents are already on payroll.
    Potential minor costs: transfer logistics, route familiarisation.
  Budget impact: frees the entire BDT 70,000 for other uses.

  Advantage: fills 3 positions at zero incremental salary cost; improves
    Lakeview's cost per delivery by right-sizing the workforce.
  Limitation: requires agents to change hub (commute, new routes);
    may face resistance. Does not create net new capacity — only
    redistributes it.

OPTION 3: INVEST IN REDUCING AGENT-SIDE HOLDS AT RIVERSIDE
  What it does: fixes the root cause of Riverside's inflated workload.
  Cost: process audit, dispatcher training, routing tool — variable,
    depends on interventions chosen (see Section 3).
  Expected capacity gain: 312 Late Dispatch holds/week = 312 freed
    attempts/week if fully resolved = 1.3 agents of capacity (Q8).

  Advantage: addresses root cause, not symptoms; benefits compound
    over time; reduces cost per delivery at Riverside permanently.
  Limitation: takes 2–4 weeks to show full effect; requires management
    commitment and operational follow-through at the hub level.

RECOMMENDED APPROACH: COMBINE ALL THREE
  Transfers (Option 2) handle the bulk of the capacity gap at zero cost.
  Hiring (Option 1) fills the remaining 2 positions the transfers
  cannot cover. The residual budget funds dispatch improvement at
  Riverside (Option 3). This is the only approach that addresses both
  Problem A and Problem B within the BDT 70,000 constraint.

WHY FIXED AGENTS, NOT VARIABLE

  The breakeven between Fixed and Variable is 25 deliveries per day:

    Fixed daily cost:      BDT 650
    Variable breakeven:    650 = 300 + 14d  →  d = 25

  At the hubs receiving new agents:
    Hillcrest W2 average:  34.6 deliveries/agent/day (Q1) → well above 25
    Riverside W2 average:  24.2 deliveries/agent/day (Q1) → just below 25

  For Hillcrest, Fixed is clearly cheaper (BDT 650 vs BDT 784/day).
  For Riverside, Fixed and Variable are near-identical at current
  workload. However, as the dispatch programme reduces holds and
  Riverside's deliveries per agent rise, Fixed becomes progressively
  cheaper. Hiring Fixed also gives a predictable cost that simplifies
  budgeting.

  The only scenario where Variable would be better is if forecast
  volumes do not materialise (Section 7). In that case, Variable agents
  cost less because their pay scales down with deliveries. But the
  forecast shows steady or growing demand across all four weeks, so
  this hedge is not worth the ongoing cost premium.

4. EXPECTED RETURN — QUANTIFIED
===============================================================================

The return comes from three sources: (a) additional deliveries from the
2 new hires, (b) additional deliveries from the 3 transferred agents
working at busier hubs, and (c) reduced waste from the dispatch fix.

SOURCE A — TWO NEW HIRES
--------------------------

Each new agent is placed at a hub operating above the planning target.
Without them, some orders cannot be attempted each day. With them, the
hub handles its full forecast volume.

  Hillcrest new agent:
    Expected attempts/day:   34  (planning target)
    Expected deliveries/day: 34 / 1.078 = 31.5  (hub's attempts-per-order, Q7)
    Deliveries/month:        31.5 × 30 = 945

  Riverside new agent:
    Expected attempts/day:   34
    Expected deliveries/day: 34 / 1.351 = 25.2  (hub's current attempts-per-order)
    Deliveries/month:        25.2 × 30 = 756

  Calculation:
    Additional deliveries from new hires = 945 + 756 = 1,701 per month

    Revenue from additional deliveries:
      1,701 × BDT 65 (avg delivery charge from orders table) = BDT 110,565

    Cost of new hires:
      2 × BDT 19,500 = BDT 39,000

    Net contribution from new hires:
      BDT 110,565 − BDT 39,000 = BDT 71,565 per month

    Payback period:
      BDT 39,000 salary / (BDT 110,565 / 30 days) = 10.6 days
      The salary of both agents is recovered in less than 11 days of
      their deliveries.

  Note: this calculation assumes the new agents operate at the planning
  target from day 1. In practice, there is a ramp-up period (see
  Section 7). A conservative estimate using 80% of the planning target
  for the first month reduces deliveries to 1,361 and revenue to
  BDT 88,465 — still well above the BDT 39,000 cost.

SOURCE B — THREE TRANSFERRED AGENTS (higher-value work)
---------------------------------------------------------

The 3 Lakeview agents are already on the company's payroll. At
Lakeview, they deliver an average of 20.4 orders/day each (Q1 W2).
When transferred to Greenfield and Hillcrest, they will work at
those hubs' higher delivery rates.

  Current output (at Lakeview):
    3 agents × 20.4 deliveries/day = 61.2 deliveries/day

  Expected output (at Greenfield/Hillcrest):
    2 agents at Greenfield: 2 × 32.0 = 64.0 deliveries/day (Q1 W2 avg)
    1 agent at Hillcrest:   1 × 34.6 = 34.6 deliveries/day (Q1 W2 avg)
    Total: 98.6 deliveries/day

  Net additional deliveries from reallocation:
    98.6 − 61.2 = 37.4 additional deliveries/day
    Monthly: 37.4 × 30 = 1,122 additional deliveries

  Revenue from additional deliveries:
    1,122 × BDT 65 = BDT 72,930

  Incremental cost: BDT 0
  (Agents' salaries are unchanged; only their location changes.)

  This is pure value creation — the same payroll expense produces
  1,122 more deliveries per month simply by putting agents where the
  demand is.

SOURCE C — RIVERSIDE DISPATCH IMPROVEMENT
-------------------------------------------

The dispatch improvement programme targets the 312 Late Dispatch holds
per week identified in Q3. Each Late Dispatch hold wastes one agent-
attempt the following day (the held order is re-attempted).

  Cost of wasted re-attempts (current state):
    Agent cost per attempt = BDT 650 / 34 = BDT 19.12
    (One Fixed agent-day at BDT 650, divided by 34 attempts at the
    planning target. This is the opportunity cost: each wasted attempt
    is one attempt not spent on a new order.)

    Weekly waste from Late Dispatch alone:
      312 × BDT 19.12 = BDT 5,965/week

    Monthly waste:
      BDT 5,965 × 4.3 = BDT 25,650/month

    Annual waste:
      BDT 25,650 × 12 = BDT 307,800/year

  If the programme reduces Late Dispatch by 50% (312 → 156/week):
    Freed attempts: 156/week × 4.3 = 671 attempts/month
    Additional deliveries: 671 / 1.075 ≈ 624 deliveries/month
    (Using the company-average attempts-per-order, since the freed
    attempts serve new orders that should not face the same hold issue.)

    Revenue from additional deliveries:
      624 × BDT 65 = BDT 40,560/month

    Agent-time savings (opportunity cost recovered):
      156 × 4.3 × BDT 19.12 = BDT 12,830/month

    Total monthly benefit:
      BDT 40,560 + BDT 12,830 = BDT 53,390

    Programme cost: BDT 0–21,000 (from budget remainder, Section 3)

  Note: the 50% reduction target is moderate. If achieved, the remaining
  156 Late Dispatch cases/week should continue to decline as the
  process improvements become routine.

5. CALCULATIONS — FORMULAS AND ASSUMPTIONS
===============================================================================

All formulas used in this analysis, with the data source for each input:

SALARY COSTS
  Fixed daily rate    = monthly_salary / 26  = 16,900 / 26 = BDT 650
                        (agent_salary table, per case notes)
  Fixed monthly cost  = 650 × 30 days = BDT 19,500
  Variable daily cost = daily_allowance + allowance_per_delivery × deliveries
                      = 300 + 14 × d
                        (agent_salary table)
  Breakeven:            650 = 300 + 14d → d = 25 deliveries/day

STAFFING GAP (from Q7)
  Attempts-per-order    = hub Assigned events / hub distinct orders
                          (order_logs + orders tables, 14-day history)
  Expected attempts     = forecast orders × attempts-per-order
                          (volume_forecast table)
  Required agents       = CEIL(expected attempts / (34 × 7))
                          (34 = 85% of 40 standard; 7 days/week)
  Gap                   = required agents − active agents

DELIVERY ESTIMATES
  Deliveries per agent per day = hub W2 average from Q1 daily table
    Greenfield: 32.0, Hillcrest: 34.6, Riverside: 24.2, Lakeview: 20.4
    (order_logs, Delivered events per agent-day, W2 only)
  New hire deliveries/day = planning target / attempts-per-order
    Hillcrest:  34 / 1.078 = 31.5
    Riverside:  34 / 1.351 = 25.2

REVENUE
  Avg delivery charge = BDT 65.03 ≈ BDT 65
    (orders table, AVG(delivery_charge) across all 22,789 orders)

DISPATCH WASTE
  Agent cost per attempt = Fixed daily rate / planning target
                         = 650 / 34 = BDT 19.12
  Weekly waste = Late Dispatch holds × cost per attempt
               = 312 × 19.12 = BDT 5,965
    (312 Late Dispatch holds from Q3, Riverside, Week 2)

ASSUMPTIONS DISTINGUISHED FROM DATA
  DATA (from the dataset):
    All salary rates, delivery charges, utilisation figures, hold counts,
    agent counts, forecast volumes, and attempts-per-order ratios.

  ASSUMPTIONS (not in the dataset):
  1. One-time hiring costs — unknown (costs tab missing). Presented as
     a variable with three scenarios (BDT 5,000 / 10,000 / 15,500).
  2. The 50% dispatch improvement target — an estimate. The actual
     reduction depends on implementation quality and root cause.
  3. New hires reach the planning target from day 1 — optimistic.
     The conservative scenario uses 80% of target for month 1.
  4. Delivery charge = revenue — simplified. The actual revenue model
     may include COD handling fees or other components not in the data.
  5. Transferred agents achieve the receiving hub's average — they may
     take 1–2 weeks to learn new routes and customer patterns.
  6. The forecast is accurate — volumes materialise as projected. If
     not, the return from new hires drops proportionally.

7. RISKS AND LIMITATIONS
===============================================================================

RISK 1: ONE-TIME COSTS CONSUME THE ENTIRE BUDGET
  If one-time costs are BDT 15,500+ per agent, the entire BDT 70,000
  goes to hiring with nothing left for the Riverside programme. This
  is still a viable plan (the 2 hires + 3 transfers fill the capacity
  gap), but Riverside's dispatch problem goes unaddressed until the
  next budget cycle.
  Mitigation: request the one-time cost data from the costs tab before
  finalising the allocation.

RISK 2: TRANSFERS ARE BLOCKED
  If cross-hub transfer is not feasible (contractual, union, or
  logistical barriers), the 3 transfer positions become 3 additional
  hires. That requires BDT 58,500 in salary + one-time costs = at
  least BDT 73,500, which exceeds the budget.
  Mitigation: prioritise transfers — they are the plan's backbone. If
  only 1 or 2 transfers succeed, hire 1 additional agent (total 3
  hires = BDT 58,500 salary), which fits the budget at low-to-mid
  one-time costs.

RISK 3: FORECAST VOLUME DOES NOT MATERIALISE
  The 4-week forecast is extrapolated from a 2-week trend. If Week 2's
  volume surge was a one-off event, actual demand may revert to Week 1
  levels (~9,700 orders/week vs ~12,500 forecast).
  Impact: the 2 new hires would be underutilised, costing BDT 39,000/
  month with lower returns. At Week 1 volumes, no hub is above target —
  even the current 62 agents are sufficient.
  Mitigation: the plan hires only 2 agents, limiting downside to
  BDT 39,000/month. If volume falls, the Riverside buffer agent can be
  redeployed. Compare with the 8-hire request at BDT 156,000/month —
  the recommended plan has 4× less downside.

RISK 4: NEW AGENT RAMP-UP IS SLOWER THAN ASSUMED
  The return calculation assumes new agents work at the planning target
  (34 attempts/day) from day 1. In practice, route learning and
  customer familiarity may take 1–2 weeks. During ramp-up, output may
  be 20–25 attempts/day.
  Impact: first-month deliveries drop by ~20%, and revenue from Source
  A falls from BDT 110,565 to ~BDT 88,450. The net return is still
  positive (BDT 88,450 − 39,000 = BDT 49,450).
  Mitigation: pair each new hire with an experienced agent for the
  first 3 days; assign well-known, high-density routes first.

RISK 5: RIVERSIDE DISPATCH FIX UNDERPERFORMS
  The 50% Late Dispatch reduction is an estimate, not a guarantee. If
  the root cause is systemic (e.g. warehouse processes outside hub
  control), improvement may be slower or smaller.
  Impact: Source C returns fall. At a 25% reduction instead of 50%,
  the monthly benefit drops from ~BDT 53,390 to ~BDT 26,695. The
  budget overall still returns positive.
  Mitigation: the dispatch programme is funded from the REMAINDER of
  the budget, not from a committed allocation. If one-time costs are
  high and the remainder is small, the company loses little if the
  programme is deferred. The 2 hires + 3 transfers stand on their own.

RISK 6: RETURN ESTIMATES USE DELIVERY CHARGE AS REVENUE
  The analysis uses the BDT 65 average delivery charge as the revenue
  per delivery. Actual revenue may differ: delivery charges might be
  partially offset by COD handling costs, refund costs on returned
  orders, or other variables not in the dataset. The direction of the
  estimate is correct, but the magnitude may vary.

OVERALL RISK PROFILE
  The plan's structure is deliberately conservative:
  - Only 2 new hires (minimum viable commitment)
  - Transfers carry zero incremental cost (reversible if needed)
  - The Riverside programme is funded from the residual, not from a
    fixed allocation
  - Even under the worst reasonable scenario (ramp-up delays + dispatch
    fix fails + volume misses by 20%), the plan returns more than its
    cost in the first month

    hiring request next quarter — and the quarter after that.
