Q10. THE RISKS
===============
What could make your plan fail, and how would you know within the
first two weeks?

Assumptions: Plan from Q8/Q9: transfer 3 Lakeview agents → Greenfield (2) + Hillcrest (1), hire 2 Fixed agents (Hillcrest + Riverside), fix Riverside's Late Dispatch; budget BDT 70,000; forecast built on only 2 weeks of order data; Brookside's 1 surplus agent is the only company-wide float.

Answer:

This plan breaks if the people do not move, the numbers do not hold, or the operations do not change — and the only way to catch it in time is to watch daily, not weekly. The biggest immediate risk is the transfers: Lakeview agents may not want to relocate, and if even one has not reported at the new hub by day 3, that hub stays short — the fix is a small commute subsidy from the dispatch budget, or hiring the position instead, both of which still fit inside BDT 70,000. The second risk is the forecast itself, which is built on just two weeks of history; if actual orders for the first forecast week land more than 15% below projection, the growth was likely a one-off, but because this plan locks in only two hires instead of eight, the downside is a fraction of what a full hiring spree would cost, and the company can delay the hires by a week to test the forecast with transfers alone. Third, Riverside's Late Dispatch problem — the single biggest source of wasted effort in the network (Q3) — may not respond to a programme; if the daily hold count has not visibly dropped by end of week 1 or attempts-per-order is still well above the company average, the programme needs a dedicated owner and a hard dispatch cut-off, and if nothing moves after two weeks, Brookside's spare agent can cover Riverside temporarily. Fourth, new hires take time to learn routes, and Hillcrest — already the most stretched hub in the company (Q2) — cannot afford a slow ramp; if a new agent is not handling a reasonable workload by day 5, pair them with a senior agent and start them on familiar routes. Fifth, as volume grows, agent-side holds could spread to hubs that are currently clean — "Agent Time Shortage" was already the leading agent-caused hold reason at three hubs (Q3), and any hub whose hold rate jumps sharply above its Week 2 baseline is about to need help, which is what Brookside's spare agent is reserved for. Sixth, Brookside's three high-return agents (Q5) quietly erode that hub's real capacity; if their return ratios stay elevated through week 1, route reassignment and a performance conversation should start before the surplus they are hiding behind disappears. No single risk here kills the plan — the danger is two or three hitting together, which would leave the company short across multiple hubs and two weeks behind; so the hard rule is simple: if two or more of these show up by end of week 1, stop and move to Scenario B (hire 5, ask Finance for an additional BDT 21,000), with the call made by 2 November so new agents start before peak forecast weeks. The uncommitted portion of the budget is not leftover — it is the plan's built-in room to react.
