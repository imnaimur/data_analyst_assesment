Q10. THE RISKS — ANALYSIS
==========================

Question
--------
What could make your plan fail, and how would you know within the
first two weeks?


THE PLAN (recap from Q8/Q9)
============================
1. Transfer 3 agents from Lakeview → Greenfield (2), Hillcrest (1).
2. Hire 1 Fixed agent for Hillcrest, 1 Fixed agent for Riverside.
3. Run a Late Dispatch improvement programme at Riverside.
Total budget: BDT 70,000.


RISK 1 — TRANSFERS FAIL (agents cannot or will not move hubs)
==============================================================

What goes wrong:
  Lakeview agents may live near that hub. Reassigning them to
  Greenfield or Hillcrest could mean a longer commute, unfamiliar
  routes, or resistance from the agents themselves. If even 1 of the
  3 transfers does not happen, Greenfield or Hillcrest stays short.

Impact:
  Each unfilled transfer = 1 hub still short by 1 agent = ~238 un-
  served attempts per week = ~222 delayed deliveries per week.
  Revenue at risk: 222 × BDT 65 = ~BDT 14,400/week.

Early-warning signal (by day 3):
  - Transfer acceptance rate. If any agent declines by end of day 2,
    the gap persists. Track: has the agent physically reported to the
    new hub and been assigned orders?

Mitigation:
  a) Offer a temporary transfer allowance (commute subsidy) from the
     Riverside programme budget — BDT 2,000–3,000 per agent covers
     transport for a month.
  b) If a transfer falls through, escalate to Scenario B from Q8:
     hire the position instead. Budget impact: 1 extra hire =
     BDT 18,200, pushing total to BDT 54,600 — still within the
     BDT 70,000 envelope.
  c) Prioritise transferring Fixed agents from Lakeview (8 available);
     their cost is the same regardless of hub, so no pay negotiation.


RISK 2 — FORECAST VOLUME DOES NOT MATERIALISE
===============================================

What goes wrong:
  The volume forecast (27 Oct – 23 Nov) is based on a trend from just
  2 weeks of data. If the Week 2 volume surge was a one-off (e.g. a
  promotional campaign that ended), actual orders could fall back to
  Week 1 levels. Then the new hires are underutilised and the company
  is paying for agents it does not need.

Impact:
  If volume drops to Week 1 levels (~9,700 orders/week vs forecast
  ~14,200/week), even current staffing is more than enough. The 2 new
  hires would cost BDT 36,400 with little to show for it.

Early-warning signal (by end of week 1, 2 Nov):
  - Actual orders vs forecast for the first week (27 Oct – 2 Nov).
    The forecast says 14,255 orders that week. If actual orders fall
    below 12,000 (>15% miss), the volume trend is weaker than
    expected.
  - Track DAILY orders by hub, not just the weekly total. A hub-level
    miss matters more than a company-wide one (the plan is hub-
    specific).

Mitigation:
  a) This is why the plan hires only 2 agents (not 8). Two Fixed
     agents at BDT 36,400 is a manageable downside. Eight at
     BDT 145,600 would be painful.
  b) If the plan used Variable agents instead (Q9, Option B), their
     cost scales down with volume: at 20 deliveries/day each agent
     costs BDT 580/day instead of BDT 650. This provides a natural
     hedge.
  c) Delay hiring by 1 week: run the first forecast week (27 Oct –
     2 Nov) with transfers only. If volume tracks forecast, hire in
     week 2. Cost of this delay: 1 week of 2 short positions =
     ~444 delayed deliveries, but it avoids committing to hires on
     an unproven forecast.


RISK 3 — RIVERSIDE'S LATE DISPATCH IS NOT FIXED
=================================================

What goes wrong:
  The dispatch improvement programme may fail to reduce Late Dispatch
  holds. Causes: the root cause is systemic (e.g. warehouse processes
  outside hub control), or the hub manager does not prioritise it, or
  the fix takes longer than 2 weeks.

Impact:
  Riverside stays at 1.35 attempts per order. The 1 buffer agent
  covers the gap for now, but the hub remains the most expensive in
  the network (BDT 27/delivery in Week 2, Q6), and if volume grows
  further, 13 agents will not be enough.
  Wasted re-attempts continue: ~312/week × BDT 19 = ~BDT 5,900/week
  in agent time on avoidable holds.

Early-warning signal (by end of week 1):
  - Daily Late Dispatch count at Riverside. Currently 312/week
    (~45/day). Track it daily. If it has not dropped below 35/day
    by end of week 1, the programme is not working.
  - Riverside's attempts-per-order for the first week. It was 1.351
    over the 14-day history. If the first forecast week is still
    above 1.25, the fix is too slow.

Mitigation:
  a) Assign a dedicated person (ops analyst or hub supervisor) to own
     the dispatch audit at Riverside. A programme with no owner fails.
  b) Start with the quickest wins: enforce a dispatch cut-off time
     (e.g. all orders dispatched by 9 AM), and flag Late Dispatch
     to the agent's supervisor the same day.
  c) If the programme fails after 2 weeks, consider transferring 1
     agent from Brookside (which has 1 spare) to Riverside. This is
     Q8 Scenario D — zero incremental cost, but removes Brookside's
     buffer.


RISK 4 — NEW HIRES DO NOT RAMP UP FAST ENOUGH
===============================================

What goes wrong:
  A new agent may take 1–2 weeks to learn routes, build customer
  familiarity, and reach the 34 attempts/day planning target. During
  ramp-up they operate at perhaps 20–25 attempts/day, which means the
  hub is still functionally short.

Impact:
  Hillcrest (95.9% utilised in Week 2, the highest in the company) is
  the most sensitive. If its new agent only handles 25 attempts/day
  for the first 2 weeks, the hub is still short by roughly 0.4 agents
  — manageable but tight.

Early-warning signal (by end of week 1):
  - New agent's daily attempt count. Pair it against the hub average.
    If the new agent is below 25 attempts/day by day 5, ramp-up is
    slower than expected.
  - Hold and return rates for new agent's orders. A new agent
    unfamiliar with routes may produce more holds (wrong addresses,
    late arrivals).

Mitigation:
  a) Pair each new hire with an experienced agent for the first 3
     days (buddy system). This costs some of the experienced agent's
     capacity but accelerates the new agent's learning.
  b) Assign the new agent to well-known, high-density routes first
     (shorter distances, fewer navigation problems).
  c) If ramp-up is slow, temporarily increase the existing agents'
     workload from 34 to 37–38 attempts/day. Week 2 data shows agents
     can handle above-40 loads for short periods (85 agent-days
     exceeded 40 attempts, Q1).


RISK 5 — AGENT-SIDE HOLDS SPREAD BEYOND RIVERSIDE
===================================================

What goes wrong:
  The other five hubs currently have agent-side hold rates of 19–26%
  (Q3). If volume rises and agents are stretched, these rates could
  increase — especially "Agent Time Shortage" holds, which were the
  top agent-side reason at Greenfield, Westwood and Lakeview. More
  holds → more re-attempts → staffing gap widens.

Impact:
  If Greenfield's hold rate doubles from 168 to 336 per week, its
  attempts-per-order rises from 1.073 to ~1.15. At peak forecast
  (2,570 orders), that is 2,956 attempts instead of 2,758 — requiring
  13 agents, not 12. The plan's 12 would then be short by 1.

Early-warning signal (by end of week 1):
  - Hub-level hold rates, especially agent-side holds, compared to
    Week 2 baselines. Any hub whose agent-side holds jump by more
    than 50% is heading for trouble.
  - "Agent Time Shortage" holds specifically, since these directly
    signal that agents are overloaded.

Mitigation:
  a) The plan already keeps Brookside at 11 agents (1 spare) as a
     company-wide buffer. If another hub's holds spike, move the
     spare agent there.
  b) Monitor daily, not weekly. Weekly averages mask a bad Wednesday
     that gets worse by Friday.


RISK 6 — HIGH-RETURN AGENTS CONTINUE TO UNDERPERFORM
=====================================================

What goes wrong:
  Q5 identified 3 agents at Brookside (A1045, A1046, A1047) with
  return ratios of 17–27%, far above the company norm. These are
  experienced agents (>1 year tenure) who consistently return orders
  instead of delivering them. If this continues unaddressed, Brookside
  loses effective capacity: it has 11 agents on paper, but 3 of them
  convert a quarter of their attempts to returns.

Impact:
  Brookside's current surplus of 1 agent masks this problem. If the
  3 high-return agents continue at their current rate, Brookside's
  effective delivery capacity is closer to 9.5 agents — turning its
  surplus into a slight deficit.

Early-warning signal (by end of week 1):
  - Daily return ratio for A1045, A1046, A1047. If it remains above
    15%, the performance issue is ongoing.

Mitigation:
  a) Investigate root cause: are these agents misassigned (wrong
     routes, wrong customer segments)? Are they cherry-picking easy
     orders? Are they on routes with genuinely difficult customers?
  b) A performance improvement plan (PIP) for agents above 20%
     return ratio with >30 days tenure.
  c) Reassign these agents to routes with higher delivery success
     rates as a diagnostic step — if returns drop, the problem was
     route-specific, not agent-specific.

SUMMARY
========
The plan's main vulnerability is not any single risk but the
compounding effect: if transfers fail AND the Riverside fix stalls AND
a new hire ramps slowly, the company is back to being short 3–4 agents
across multiple hubs — close to the hub managers' original request for
8, and now 2 weeks behind.

The defences are layered:
  1. Keep Brookside's spare agent as a float (not transferred out).
  2. Monitor daily (not weekly) with clear thresholds.
  3. Have a pre-agreed escalation: if 2+ risks materialise by end of
     week 1, convert to Scenario B (hire 5 agents, total BDT 91,000 —
     which needs an additional BDT 21,000 budget release from Finance).
  4. The BDT 70,000 budget itself is a risk hedge: by spending only
     BDT 36,400 on guaranteed salary, BDT 33,600 remains flexible for
     whatever the first week reveals.

The worst outcome is not spending too little — it is spending too much
too soon on hiring, before understanding whether the real problem is
capacity or operations. This plan gives the company two weeks to learn
the answer while covering the most urgent gaps.
