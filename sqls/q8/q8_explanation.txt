Q8. THE HIRING REQUEST — STRATEGIC ASSESSMENT
===============================================

Question
--------
Hub managers have asked for 8 new agents to handle the forecast.
Would you approve the request? Make your case hub by hub, with costs.


===============================================================================
1. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
===============================================================================

Decision: PARTIALLY APPROVE — hire 2 agents, not 8.

The hiring request overstates the company's actual need by a factor of
four. The data tells a clear story:

  Company-wide active agents:    62
  Company-wide peak requirement: 63  (from Q7, worst forecast week)
  Net gap:                       +1

The request for 8 new agents treats every hub as if it must solve its
own staffing problem in isolation. In reality, the six hubs form a
connected network within Metro City. Two hubs (Lakeview and Brookside)
have significant surplus capacity, while three (Greenfield, Hillcrest,
Riverside) are genuinely short. The sixth (Westwood) is balanced.

The critical distinction this analysis makes is between:

  (a) CAPACITY shortages — hubs that genuinely do not have enough agents
      for the forecasted volume (Greenfield, Hillcrest). These need
      agents, whether hired or transferred.

  (b) OPERATIONAL inefficiencies masquerading as capacity problems —
      Riverside, where 64.4% of holds are agent-side (Q3) and each
      order requires 1.351 attempts instead of the ~1.075 company
      average. Riverside's apparent staffing gap of +1 would become a
      surplus of -1 if its dispatch operations were fixed.

Approving all 8 hires would cost BDT 156,000 per month in salary alone,
add agents to hubs that already have too many, and leave Riverside's
underlying dispatch problem untouched.

The recommended plan — transfer 3 existing agents from surplus hubs and
hire 2 — fills every gap at BDT 39,000 per month, saving BDT 117,000
per month (75%) compared to the full request.

  874 total holds in Week 2 — more than all other 5 hubs combined

  Week 2 cost per delivery: BDT 27.00 — the 2nd highest (Q6).

  produces the worst customer outcomes.

  agent next quarter.

  - 312 Late Dispatch events per week = orders not getting out of the

  - 132 Not Attempted events = orders assigned to agents but never

  312/week to <50/week within 2–4 weeks.

  If the dispatch fix succeeds, Riverside moves from needing 13 agents
  to needing 11 — a surplus of 2. The buffer agent can then be
  reassigned to provide cover elsewhere, or the position can be allowed
  to lapse if hired on a Variable contract.

  Monthly cost of the buffer hire: BDT 19,500 (Fixed).

4. OPERATIONAL CONSIDERATIONS
===============================================================================

INFLATED UTILISATION: WHERE "BUSY" DOES NOT MEAN "PRODUCTIVE"
--------------------------------------------------------------

Riverside's 90.8% Week 2 utilisation (Q2) appears to justify more
agents. But utilisation measures attempts, not outcomes. When 18.4% of
a hub's attempts are avoidable re-attempts from agent-side holds, the
utilisation figure overstates productive workload.

Decomposing Riverside's Week 2 utilisation:

  Total W2 attempts:                  3,051
  Agent-side holds (re-attempts):       563  (18.4% of attempts)
  Productive attempts (first-time     2,488  (81.6% of attempts)
    or customer-side retries):

  Productive utilisation:             2,488 / (12 × 40 × 7)
                                    = 2,488 / 3,360 = 74.0%

  At 74% productive utilisation, Riverside is not overloaded — it is
  spinning its wheels. The apparent gap of +1 agent disappears entirely
  when unproductive work is removed.

No other hub has this problem at scale. At the remaining hubs, agent-
side holds are 19–26% of total holds (which themselves are much smaller
than Riverside's), so the gap between headline utilisation and
productive utilisation is negligible.

HOW FIXING AGENT-SIDE HOLDS REDUCES WORKLOAD WITHOUT ADDING HEADCOUNT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Each Late Dispatch hold at Riverside creates a chain:
  Day 1: Order assigned → dispatched late → Hold recorded → re-attempt
          scheduled for Day 2
  Day 2: Same order re-assigned → another attempt (if dispatched on time,
          delivered; if not, held again)

Eliminating 1 Late Dispatch event saves:
  - 1 wasted attempt on Day 2 (agent time freed for a new order)
  - Reduced risk of a return if the customer gets frustrated by delays
  - Improved delivery charge collection (held orders delay revenue)

At 312 Late Dispatch events per week, fixing this one issue frees up
312 agent-attempts per week — equivalent to adding 312 / (34 × 7) =
1.3 agents of capacity. That is MORE than the +1 gap Riverside asked
to fill by hiring.

Similarly, "Not Attempted" (132/week) and "Agent Time Shortage"
(119/week) suggest scheduling and routing problems, not headcount
problems. An agent assigned an order they never attempt represents a
double waste: the agent's time that day plus the re-attempt tomorrow.

HUBS WITH UNUSED CAPACITY
---------------------------

Lakeview (56.2% utilisation, 3 surplus agents):
  Lakeview's 3 surplus agents represent BDT 58,500/month in
  underutilised payroll. Transferring them to Greenfield and Hillcrest
  turns dead cost into productive capacity. Post-transfer, Lakeview
  retains 8 agents — still sufficient for its peak requirement of 8.

Brookside (71.5% utilisation, 1 surplus agent):
  Brookside's surplus is smaller and should be held as a company-wide
  reserve. If any hub experiences an unexpected volume spike or an agent
  departure, Brookside can lend its spare agent temporarily.

  Note: Brookside's effective surplus is partly offset by its 3 high-
  return agents (Q5). If those agents' performance improves, Brookside's
  real surplus is closer to 2.

RISKS OF HIRING TOO EARLY VS TOO LATE
---------------------------------------

Hiring too early (approving 8 now):
  - BDT 117,000/month in avoidable salary costs (vs hiring 2).
  - Agents at surplus hubs (Lakeview, Brookside) sit idle, demoralised.
  - Riverside's dispatch problem is ignored; it will worsen as volume
    grows, leading to yet another hiring request next quarter.
  - If the forecast is wrong (volume returns to Week 1 levels), the
    company carries 8 unnecessary agents at BDT 156,000/month.

Hiring too late (rejecting the request entirely):
  - Greenfield and Hillcrest genuinely need more capacity NOW. At 88.6%
    and 95.9% utilisation in Week 2, these hubs are already above target.
    The forecast projects higher volumes.
  - Understaffing leads to: agents exceeding the 40-attempt daily
    standard → fatigue → more holds and returns → cascading demand on
    subsequent days → customer dissatisfaction.
  - Each unfilled position at Greenfield/Hillcrest means ~238
    unhandled attempts per week (~222 delayed deliveries, BDT ~14,400
    revenue at risk per position per week).

The recommended plan (transfer 3 + hire 2) avoids both extremes: it
fills every genuine gap immediately while keeping costs minimal and
directing management attention to the root cause at Riverside.

ASSUMPTIONS AND LIMITATIONS
-----------------------------

1. One-time hiring costs (onboarding, training, equipment) are
   referenced in the "costs" tab of cost_sheet.xlsx but this data is
   not in the dataset. The costs above are salary only. One-time costs
   would increase the total but also strengthen the case against
   unnecessary hires.

2. Transfers assume agents can be reassigned across hubs within the
   same city. If cross-hub transfer is infeasible (contractual or
   logistical barriers), the 3 transfer positions become 3 additional
   hires, raising the total to 5 at BDT 97,500/month. Even in this
   worst case, 5 is still fewer than the requested 8.

3. The forecast is based on a trend from 2 weeks of data. If volumes
   do not materialise, even 2 hires may be excessive. The plan
   mitigates this by keeping the committed spend small (BDT 39,000/
   month) and retaining flexibility.

4. Planning capacity is 34 attempts per agent per day (85% of the
   40-attempt standard), 7 days per week = 238 attempts per agent per
   week. Required agents are rounded UP (CEIL) so no hub is planned
   above target.

5. All utilisation and gap calculations count only Active agents.
   Inactive agents left at the end of Week 1 and are excluded from
   Week 2 and forecast calculations.

6. The average delivery charge is BDT 65 per order (from the orders
   table), used where revenue impact is referenced. This is the
   company's direct revenue; it does not include COD handling or other
   indirect revenue.
