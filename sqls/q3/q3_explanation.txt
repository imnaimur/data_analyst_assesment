Q3. HOLD REASONS - EXPLANATION
==============================

Question
--------
For Week 2, find each hub's total holds, the share that are agent-side,
and its top two hold reasons.


Assumptions
-----------
1. Week 2 = 20 to 26 October 2026.
2. A hold = one row in order_logs with status = 'Hold'.
   An order held on two different days counts as two holds.
3. Agent-side reasons (from the case notes):
   Late Dispatch, Not Attempted, Agent Time Shortage.
   All other reasons (Customer Unreachable, Customer Rescheduled,
   Address Incomplete) are customer-side.
4. "Top two hold reasons" is read as the top two AGENT-SIDE reasons,
   because those are the ones the hub can fix itself.
5. A hold belongs to the hub of the agent who made the attempt
   (order_logs has no hub column, so the hub comes from the agents table).
6. If two reasons have the same count, both are shown together
   (e.g. "Agent Time Shortage (16) / Not Attempted (16)") instead of
   picking one at random.


How the query works
-------------------
The query is built in small steps using WITH (CTEs). Each step does one job
and the next step uses its result.

Step 1 - hub_reasons
  Joins hubs -> agents -> order_logs to get each hold's hub.
  WHERE keeps only Hold rows in Week 2.
  GROUP BY hub_name, hold_reason counts holds per hub per reason.
  Result: one row per hub + reason, e.g. "Riverside | Late Dispatch | 312".

Step 2 - hub_totals
  Collapses step 1 to one row per hub.
  SUM(holds)                      -> total holds
  SUM(holds) FILTER (WHERE reason
    IN (agent-side reasons))      -> agent-side holds

Step 3 - ranked_reasons
  Keeps only agent-side reasons and ranks them inside each hub:
    RANK() OVER (PARTITION BY hub_name ORDER BY holds DESC)
  PARTITION BY hub_name -> numbering restarts for every hub
  ORDER BY holds DESC   -> biggest count gets rank 1
  RANK (not ROW_NUMBER) is used so tied reasons get the same rank
  (1, 2, 2) and neither is dropped.

Step 4 - top_reasons
  Turns the ranked rows into one row per hub with two columns.
  STRING_AGG(..., ' / ') joins tied reasons into one cell.
  FILTER (WHERE reason_rank = 1) -> top_reason_1
  FILTER (WHERE reason_rank = 2) -> top_reason_2
  hold_reason || ' (' || holds || ')' builds labels like "Late Dispatch (312)".

Final SELECT
  Joins hub_totals with top_reasons on hub_name and calculates:
    agent_side_pct = agent_side_holds * 100.0 / total_holds
  100.0 (not 100) forces decimal division; integer / integer would
  drop the decimals.

Brookside  |   186 |         49 |   26.3% | Late Dispatch (17)       | Agent Time Shortage (16) / Not Attempted (16)

Check: total holds across all hubs = 1,679, matching all Week 2 Hold rows.


Key findings
------------
Week 2 produced 1,679 holds across the six hubs, and more than half of
them came from one place: Riverside, with 874. No other hub reached 200.

What sets Riverside apart is who caused them. At the other hubs, at
least three in four holds start with the customer: they couldn't be reached,
asked for another day, or gave an incomplete address. At Riverside it's
the reverse. 64% of its holds were agent-side. Late Dispatch alone caused
312, more than the total holds at any other hub, and Not Attempted added
another 132. Riverside accounts for 563 of the network's 740 agent-side
holds (76%).

This explains what showed up earlier. In Q1, more than one in four
Riverside attempts ended in a hold, and in Q2 the hub ran at 91% of its
standard workload. The two are linked: every held order comes back for
another attempt the next day, so part of Riverside's heavy workload is
repeat work it created itself.

The other five hubs look much alike. Agent-side holds are 19-26% of
their total, and each agent-side reason accounts for only 7 to 20 holds
over the whole week. Late Dispatch tops the agent-side list at Brookside
and Hillcrest; Agent Time Shortage tops it at Greenfield, Westwood and
Lakeview. At these small numbers the ranking is a hint, not a pattern,
and at Brookside and Hillcrest second place is a tie.

So Riverside's hold problem is about how its orders get dispatched, not
about how many agents it has. Fixing that would cut its holds, free up
attempts, and lighten its workload before any agent is added.
